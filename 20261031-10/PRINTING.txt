Cross & Lace — three-part shoelace-practice saddle kit card.
1. Print bed-flat at 100% millimeter scale. PLA/PETG, .16–.20 mm layers, three perimeters. No supports intended. Adult craft/teaching model; small parts and cord, not a certified children's toy or wearable shoe.
2. Snip twelve accessible bed-level runners about 1.6 mm outside the pieces. Trim residual nubs without filing functional tongues or eye bores. Keep sole and two pierced eye rails.
3. Export the sole and one rail first as a calibration pair. Source measured sheet 1.6 mm and sockets 4.5×1.700012 mm; tongue 4.4 plus total allowance .10 reconstructs that section. Default .15 is proposed and printer/material dependent. Dry-fit then bond all four tongue seats with filament-compatible adhesive. Cure fully. Shoulders rest on sole; tongue tips stop .05 mm above bed. No screws, magnets or bearings.
4. Insert each rail DOWN into its two matching sole slots, eyes at the top and long axes parallel. Both rails have the same orientation. Lowering is unobstructed; do not try to snap through material. Glue is required for fixed retention, not a claimed friction lock.
5. Supply one soft shoelace or cord, diameter no more than eye bore minus 1 mm (default 3 mm maximum). Aglet must pass the bore too. Starting at the toe-end pair, feed equal ends from outside through each rail. Bring the outside ends UP and OVER the rail rims, cross above the clear instep gap, lower on the opposite inside, then feed the next opposing eye from inside to outside. Repeat toward the heel with slack turns; the upright eyes differ from flat shoe eyelets. Tie a bow above the last pair, gently untie and relace. Alternate straight/ladder routing is possible with slack over-rim turns; the model does not force a pattern.
6. Start with approximately 500–700 mm lace; final length depends on knot, tail preference and route. Trim/secure fraying ends safely. Printed eye rims may need gentle deburring; do not pull hard. This is a lacing/bow practice saddle, not a load-bearing clamp or shoe last. Physical friction, bow tying, strength and printing are untested. The 3D view shows printed pieces only; no cord simulation or included lace is implied.
7. Width profile, 3–5 eye pairs, pitch, bore diameter, instep spacing and rail height alter actual geometry. All eyes remain at least 2.5 mm from the top, 5.5 mm from rail ends and at least 3 mm apart. External cord remains removable; the fixed rails are bonded.
8. Personalization engraves the outboard sole lug only. PNG/JPEG/simple SVG/text/icons become a 20×20 sampled heightfield cutter (2D-to-relief, not 3D object reconstruction). ASCII/binary STL uses exact validated closed convex flat-bottom mesh triangles. Unsupported concave/open/nonmanifold meshes are rejected, never replaced by convex hulls or claimed repaired. Engraving .4–.6 mm leaves ≥1.8 mm backing and cannot touch slots/eyes. All processing local. STL stores neither units nor colors.
9. Reference: White Cat Kit Card, Mike Kasberg, Printables 865769; https://github.com/mkasberg/white-cat-kit-card ; CC BY 4.0 https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/ . Original measured-interface reconstruction; raw source STL not bundled. Shoe-lacing geometry and card layout are new, not the source spacecraft.
10. No physical printing, assembly, cord friction, strength or learning outcome has been tested. Computational mesh, fit, insertion, tab release and cord-clearance checks do not substitute for a physical trial. Calibrate and inspect the slicer first.
