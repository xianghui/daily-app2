Ramp & Release — a four-part inclined-plane friction-study kit card.
1. Print at 100% millimeters, PLA/PETG, .16–.20 mm layers, three perimeters. The connected card, ramp, two supports and sled all touch Z=0 in print orientation; no supports intended. Card 200–230 × 175–200 mm. Use individual parts for small beds.
2. Snip SIXTEEN bed-level runners 1.6 mm outside the part edges; trim nubs and discard carrier. Keep all four parts. Runners land on solid flanks away from sockets, top-edge tongues and the sled's smooth contact face.
3. Reference White Cat Kit Card measured sheet planes 0/1.6 mm and four socket loops 4.5 × 1.700012 mm. Tongue 4.4 × sheet1.6 plus .10 TOTAL allowance reconstructs that fit; default .15 and .10–.35 range are proposed printer/material fits, not universal. Print one support and the ramp as a fit trial. Deburr elephant foot; never force a tight socket.
4. Place the supports upright on their long horizontal edges, parallel and spaced (lane width−10) mm center-to-center. Both slope upward in the same direction. Lower the ramp NORMAL to its slope over all four tongues, with the signature lug outboard. Each tongue stops .05 mm below the top face. Dry-fit, then bond FOUR fixed seats with suitable adhesive and let cure. The supports must be bonded: loose clearance seats are not positive retention.
5. Place the sled on the ramp's clear center lane. It intentionally lifts out and can slide off the lower end: there is no keeper, stop or claim of captivity. Keep the small kit in a shallow tray on a desk, away from children. No extra bearings, fasteners or hardware; adhesive required. Both ramp and sled first-layer faces are their print-bed surfaces, but the ramp running face is its top skin. Smooth/deburr the sled underside; do not sand functional tongues.
6. Tilt experiment: regenerate different 12–24 degree ramps, or compare sled silhouettes/materials at one slope. At the ideal sliding threshold mu_static = tan(angle); the displayed value is a calculated threshold, NOT a measured friction coefficient or prediction of motion. FDM texture, contamination, weight and material matter. No physical printing, assembly, strength or sliding test was performed. The slider in assembled view only moves the preview sled along a checked lane; it is not a physics simulation and does not change exports.
7. Ramp length is measured ALONG the slope, not its horizontal projection. Low underside height is 8 mm; high underside height = 8 + length × sin(angle). Support contact faces are matched to that inclination. Lane width34–42, slope length48–60, sled diameter12–16, ramp thickness2.4–3.2 and support sheet1.6–2 mm. Three flat sled profiles: round, faceted hex and oval; same nominal diameter need not mean equal mass or friction.
8. Local text/icons/PNG/JPEG/simple SVG engrave only an outboard signature lug, 0.4–0.6 mm deep with ≥1.8 mm solid backing, clear of the running lane and four slots. 20×20 sampled threshold/grayscale height field is 2D-to-relief conversion, not exact tracing or full 3D photo reconstruction. Bright holes stay unengraved. Accepted ASCII/binary STL is Boolean-subtracted using its exact triangles: only one closed outward-wound convex broad-flat-bottom mesh is supported. Unsupported concavities, shells, branches and overhangs are rejected, never silently convex-hulled. Unit metadata is absent in STL: explicitly confirm mm, select up axis before attaching, then scale/rotate/place. Files stay local and bounded portable payloads restore through JSON/named saves. STL contains no preview colors.
