Bridge & Bend — eight-part flexure comparison kit card.
1. Print bed-flat at 100% millimeter scale. PLA/PETG, .16–.20 mm layers, three perimeters; strips should be solid with paths running along their length. No supports intended. Not a calibrated test instrument or children's toy.
2. Snip all 32 accessible bed-level runners about1.6 mm outside the pieces. Trim the residual nubs, not the tongues, cradle tops or test strips. Keep base, two trestles, central stop blade, loading shoe and three strips.
3. Export base and one trestle first to calibrate. Source measured sheet1.6 mm and sockets4.5×1.700012 mm; tongue4.4 plus total allowance.10 reconstructs that fit. Default.15 is proposed, printer/material dependent. Insert each trestle down from above into its two slots; shoulders stop on the base. Tongue tips stop.05 mm above the bed. Bond these four fixed seats with filament-compatible adhesive; cure before use.
4. Insert the central stop blade down into the central slot and bond its single tongue. The blade top is the selected central travel gap below the underside of an unloaded strip. Five glued seats, three upright pieces. No screws, magnets, bearings or other hardware; adhesive is required.
5. Lay ONE strip lengthwise in the two shallow open cradles, with equal end overhangs. Store other strips beside the model (the assembled view does so too). Center the free loading shoe on top. Gently press until the strip first touches the central stop; do not force past contact. Lift the shoe, replace the strip, compare the three widths. Open cradles and loose shoe intentionally allow tool-free swaps; they are NOT captive. Keep fingers clear; don't flick or launch strips.
6. All three strips share length and thickness; widths are w,w+2,w+4. For a linear, simply supported beam with a central point load and identical elastic material, relative stiffness is proportional to width and thickness cubed divided by span cubed. Actual shoe footprint, broad support contact, print anisotropy and material creep differ from the ideal. Displayed strain estimate6*t*gap/span² is geometric guidance only, NOT force calibration, elastic-limit proof, fatigue or safety testing. Start with a very gentle small deflection and discard cracked/whitened strips.
7. Assembled view shows UNDEFORMED parts and stored spare strips; no simulated bending is claimed. Export always uses the bed-flat geometry, not assembled overlap. Printed stop gap and supports are real; computational checks cover contacts, insertion and an idealized deflection envelope, not physical behavior.
8. Personalization engraves only the outboard base lug: PNG/JPEG/simple SVG/text/icons become20×20 sampled heightfield cutters (2D-to-relief, not full3D photo reconstruction). ASCII/binary STL uses exact validated closed convex flat-bottom mesh triangles; concave/open/nonmanifold/unsupported input is rejected, never replaced by a convex hull. Engraving.4–.6 mm leaves≥1.8 mm base backing. All processing local. STL stores neither units nor colors.
9. Reference: White Cat Kit Card, Mike Kasberg, Printables865769; https://github.com/mkasberg/white-cat-kit-card ; CC BY4.0 https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/ . Original measured-interface parametric reconstruction; no raw source STL bundled. Geometry and card layout are new, not the original spacecraft.
10. Physical printing, fit, bending forces, strength, fatigue and assembly have NOT been tested. Calibrate with the actual printer/material; inspect slicer and use adult supervision for small parts.
