Corners & Concord — five-part triangle angle-sum kit card.
1. Print bed-flat, 100% millimeter scale, PLA/PETG, .4 mm nozzle, .16–.20 mm layers, three walls. No supports intended. Base has a sheet-thick floor plus a sheet-high rim (2× sheet total); other parts are sheet-thick. Print the base and board as a fit trial first. Clearance .10–.35 mm TOTAL is printer/material dependent, not universal.
2. Clip FOURTEEN bed-aligned runners at least 1.5 mm outboard of clean part edges. Four on base, four on board sides (never tongues), two on each sector pickup ear. File residual nubs flush. Wear eye protection and supervise snips/small parts.
3. Set base flat. Lower triangle-board tongues into its two back sockets, matching the engraving-forward orientation. Shoulders rest on floor top. Compatible adhesive is required in BOTH sockets; let cure. Printed tongues are 4.50 mm wide; sockets (4.50+gap) by (sheet+gap). No unverified friction retention is promised.
4. Match one/two/three dots to the lower-left/lower-right/apex corners of the upright triangle. The three loose sectors carry exactly these nominal angles; truncated triangle tips remove 0.8 mm along each edge, not its angle. Sectors have a 5 mm inner hole and half-clearance edge inset for printable, removable tips. These deliberate gaps are not missing angle.
5. Lower sector A into the right end of the semicircular tray, then B, then C counterclockwise. The middle of each sector points through its own ear notch. The three nominal angles total 180 degrees. Their exposed radial edges line up with a straight diameter; small calibrated gaps remain. Lift by the projecting ears to repeat. No moving captive joint, hardware or force-fit is involved. Do not glue the sectors.
6. Triangles vary through base 54–68 mm and two independent 35–65 degree angles; apex is 180-A-B (50–110 degrees). All three sectors rebuild from these angles. Radius 20–26 mm changes the real tray and pieces. One/two/three recessed dots remain visible. This is a geometric model of the angle-sum theorem, not a metrology instrument or a proof for curved surfaces.
7. PNG/JPEG/SVG and text/icons are actual 0.4–0.6 mm engravings in the front apron, centered at Y=-28 mm, safely outside the tray and back sockets. A bounded 20×20 threshold or grayscale height grid retains sampled bright holes with continuous backing. This is 2D-to-relief, not 3D reconstruction. Only engraved marks are offered to protect bed-flat fit. STL stores no color.
8. ASCII/binary STL integrates as an exact Boolean engraving, not unchanged re-export or hull replacement. Confirm millimeter units. Up to 1,500 triangles, one convex closed outward-wound broad-flat-bottom solid is supported. Concave/hollow/overhanging/self-intersecting/nonmanifold input is explicitly rejected, not repaired. Scale footprint/depth, rotate Z and place within the protected apron. User files remain local; portable JSON includes bounded payloads.
9. Reference: Mike Kasberg, White Cat Kit Card / Printables 865769, github.com/mkasberg/white-cat-kit-card, CC BY 4.0. Measured 1.60 mm sheet, 4.50×1.700012 mm sockets, low carrier planes 0.21–0.22 mm. Original triangle/sector/tray reconstruction retains 4.50 mm tongues and measured default sheet, with proposed calibrated sockets and stronger 0.4–0.6 mm runners. No source mesh/artwork redistributed. Source artifacts were not used as printable output.
10. ZIP contains connected-card and five clean-part STLs plus current fits and these instructions. Connected-card and de-tabbed plate are different exports. Assembled/exploded views use the same solids, but STL remains bed-flat. Computational checks are NOT physical print testing: printing, snipping, glue strength, fit, wear and actual classroom use are untested.
