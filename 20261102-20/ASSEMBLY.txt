Maxwell & Unwind — seven-part twin-cord winding-wheel kit.
1. Print the connected card or clean parts at 100% mm scale, PLA/PETG, .4 mm nozzle, .16–.20 mm layers, three walls. All seven pieces bed-flat with no supports intended. Card 230–260 × 300–330 mm: use separate clean parts if your bed is smaller. First print an axle and one wheel as a fit trial. Total socket clearance .10–.35 mm is proposed for calibration, not universal.
2. Snip TWENTY-FOUR accessible bed-aligned runners, at least 1.5 mm outside each part. Four on the base, each pillar, lintel and axle; two on each wheel. File nubs without thinning the axle or tongues. Snips, compatible adhesive and TWO equal soft nonstretch cords about 180 mm long, diameter .3–.6 mm, are required. Small knots must pass through or tie at the 1.4 mm axle and 2.4 mm lintel eyes. No bearings or purchased metal hardware.
3. Lower the two pillar tongues into the base slots. Shoulders seat on the base, tongues reach the bed. Glue and cure. Lower the cord lintel over both top tongues and glue these two seats. Four gantry joints total. Keep the frame square; it is a lightweight model, not load-bearing.
4. Feed the rectangular axle through both wheel sockets before adding cords. Set the two discs at the chosen center-plane separation, symmetrical about the axle midpoint, and perpendicular to the shaft. Glue BOTH disc/axle joints and cure. Socket clearance is not a friction lock; the rectangular interfaces prevent nominal free rotation after bonding. In the preview only, wheel centers are fixed at the requested spacing: use a ruler or temporary spacers when bonding. There are no built-in axial shoulders.
5. Anchor the two cords to the axle's end eyes with secure knots. Tie the upper ends through the corresponding lintel eyes at equal lengths. Adjust knots so the suspended wheel cannot touch the base, pillars or lintel. Wind both cords in the SAME sense and equal turns on the outer axle lands. Keep cords outside the two wheel faces. Never wind across a wheel. Rotate the axle gently to raise the wheel; release at a safe height, catch before the end and unwind if cords cross. Do not use long loops around fingers or body.
6. Maxwell-wheel interaction: a coupled two-disc rotor descends as two cords unwind; its stored rotation may rewind and lift it. This rectangular 4.50 × sheet axle deliberately pulses effective winding radius. No timing, automatic rewind, energy-loss, knot retention, fatigue or physical print test was performed. Two cords, not an internal captive joint, suspend it. Assembled/exploded poses are static fit illustrations; straight cord ghosts are estimated and do not simulate wound paths.
7. A 0.4–0.6 mm Boolean engraving personalizes the front base apron at Y=-19 mm, clear of the gantry and moving wheel. PNG/JPEG/SVG → bounded 20×20 threshold/grayscale height field; bright holes retain backing. This is 2D-to-relief, not full 3D reconstruction. Text and bundled icons use the same local raster path. All art leaves >=1 mm continuous backing. STL cannot store preview colour.
8. ASCII/binary STL import uses actual mesh triangles, Boolean-subtracted into the apron. Confirm mm because STL has no units. One closed convex outward-wound broad-flat-bottom solid, <=1500 triangles and .2–250 mm/axis. Concave, open, hollow, overhanging, disconnected or self-intersecting sources are explicitly unsupported, never silently repaired or replaced with a hull. Use XY footprint, independent Z depth, rotation and placement controls. Inputs stay local and bounded payloads travel in JSON.
9. Reference: Mike Kasberg, White Cat Kit Card, Printables 865769, github.com/mkasberg/white-cat-kit-card, CC BY 4.0. Actually parsed source: 1.60 mm sheet, 4.50 × 1.700012 mm socket loops, low carrier planes .21–.22 mm. Original reconstruction preserves 4.50 mm tongue/axle width and 1.60 mm default sheet, adds proposed calibrated fit and .4–.6 mm removable runners. No source spacecraft artwork or raw mesh redistributed.
10. ZIP contains connected card plus seven clean-part STLs and these numbered instructions with current fits. Flat card, de-tabbed plate and assembled views use the same solids, but exports always use bed-flat orientation. Computational tests check solids, snips, interference and insertion, NOT physical printing or cord dynamics. All physical fit, snipping force, glue strength, balance, descent and rewind remain untested.
