Square & Roll — four-part square-wheel catenary kit card.
1. Print flat at 100% millimeter scale. PLA/PETG, .4 mm nozzle, .16–.20 mm layers, three walls. No supports intended; wheel is thicker (8–12 mm) than base/rails (1.6–2.4 mm). Print a source-derived tongue/socket coupon by printing a rail and base before the whole kit. Clearance .10–.35 mm TOTAL is only a printer/material starting point.
2. Clip SIXTEEN bed-aligned runners, four per part, 1.5 mm outside clean edges. File the stubs flush, especially wheel side edges. Runners attach to rail root ends, not their rolling profiles or tongues. Snips, file and adhesive required; no hardware. Small parts and snipping eye-protection caution.
3. Lay signature base flat. Stand the two identical rails on their root shoulders. Lower each pair of 4.50 mm tongues together into its matching socket pair. Tongue tips end flush with the underside; root shoulders rest on the top. Bond all FOUR joints, keeping both rails parallel and upright until cured. These are clearance seats, not snap retention.
4. Stand the thick square wheel vertically across both rails, centered in Y. Its plain square edges are functional: do not chamfer them or scale the wheel separately. Roll slowly along the central two humps while gently guiding its side faces. The wheel is NOT captive, has no axle and can fall off the ends or tip sideways. This is a hands-on rolling geometry demonstration, not an unattended vehicle. No force or friction prediction.
5. A square of side 2a rolls over four inverted-catenary humps y = B + a(sqrt(2) - cosh(u/a)), |u| <= a asinh(1). Hump pitch = 2a asinh(1). The ideal center remains at baseTop + B + a sqrt(2); orientation is -atan(sinh(u/a)) modulo quarter turns. Each hump uses 96 segments, with a conservative profile chord-error bound displayed in the studio. Preview poses are kinematic, not a dynamic physics simulation.
6. Choose wheel side to co-regenerate the REAL catenary profile. Wheel width and edge margin determine rail center spacing. Socket clearance controls assembly only, not rolling contact. Shapes change the base outline, never the square's rolling edges. Do not expect polygonal profiles or printer steps to roll perfectly smoothly.
7. Personalization engraves the protected front base apron: 0.4–0.6 mm depth with >=1 mm backing. PNG/JPEG/SVG and text/icons use a bounded 20x20 sampled threshold/grayscale surface. Bright holes remain unengraved. This is 2D-to-relief, not full 3D photo reconstruction. STL does not store preview colors.
8. ASCII/binary STL uses the actual uploaded triangles as a Boolean engraving tool. Explicitly confirm mm units. One closed convex outward-wound flat-bottom solid, <=1500 triangles, .2–250 mm per source axis. Concave/open/nonmanifold/overhanging/self-intersecting meshes are explicitly unsupported, never repaired or replaced by a hull. Up axis is selected before attaching; XY footprint, Z depth, rotation and placement are independent.
9. Reference: Mike Kasberg, White Cat Kit Card / Printables 865769, https://github.com/mkasberg/white-cat-kit-card, CC BY 4.0. Fresh mesh cross-sections measured 1.60 mm sheet, 4.50 x 1.700 mm slots and low runners. Original parametric reconstruction retains sheet and tongue widths; calibrated slots, stronger .4–.6 mm runners, catenary rails, square roller and frame packing are redesigned. Raw third-party mesh and spacecraft artwork are not bundled.
10. ZIP includes connected card, four clean-part STLs, design JSON and these instructions. Computational checks cover serialized mesh manifoldness, bed support, rail insertion, runner release, rolling-envelope intersections and matching track/wheel proportions. Physical print, fit, friction, glue strength and rolling smoothness have NOT been tested.
