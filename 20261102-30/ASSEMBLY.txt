Simplex & Tension — three-strut tensegrity prism kit card.
1. Print card bed-flat at 100% millimeters, PLA/PETG, .4 mm nozzle, .16–.20 mm layers, three walls. All parts 1.6–2.4 mm thick; runners .4–.6 mm. No supports or glue intended. Required external material: .5–.8 mm low-stretch soft cord, scissors, a ruler and temporary tape or helper hands. Small knots and snips require care. NOT load-bearing. Physical printing, tension stability and knot grip NOT tested.
2. Snip SIXTEEN runners 1.3 mm outside part bounding edges. File stubs, preserving eyes. Separate three identical two-eye struts and a notched cord/threading shuttle. The shuttle assists winding and feeding cord; it is NOT a structural member and stays beside the assembled prism. Prefer clean individual strut STLs if tab filing is inconvenient.
3. Label lower endpoints A0,A1,A2 counterclockwise on a triangle and upper endpoints B0,B1,B2 counterclockwise on a triangle rotated +30 degrees. Connect each strut A_i to B_(i+1 modulo 3). Never connect corresponding A_i/B_i with a strut. Three bars remain physically separated. Rest lower eyes around a temporary paper triangle of chosen radius; tape helps while tying. The preview is an ideal tie-point pose, not an elastic/tension simulation.
4. Tie lower rim A0-A1-A2-A0, upper rim B0-B1-B2-B0, then side stays A0-B0, A1-B1, A2-B2: NINE segments total. Adjust knots iteratively and gently remove temporary tape. Target straight tie-point distances: rim sqrt(3)*R; side stay sqrt(H^2+(2R*sin15deg)^2); strut eye-to-eye sqrt(H^2+(2R*sin75deg)^2). Current values are shown in studio/ZIP JSON. Add at least 35 mm per segment for tying, and adjust for cord routing through finite eye thickness and knots. These are centerline starting lengths, not exact cut lengths or guaranteed tension settings. Leave tails, then trim only after balancing. Never force a bent strut.
5. The +30 degree top rotation and cyclic offset +1 have positive analytic self-stress: bar force density -1, side stay +1, rim +1/sqrt(3). This establishes ideal geometric equilibrium only, NOT nonlinear stability, stiffness, knot behavior or physical operation. Do not put objects on it. A helper is recommended. If it collapses, release tension and recheck indexing, slack, knots and lengths; do not overtighten printed eyes. No moving fit or universal tolerance is claimed. The full nine-string diagram is preview-only external cord; STL exports only real flat plastic parts.
6. Personalize the shuttle with PNG/JPEG/simple SVG, text/icons or exact verified ASCII/binary STL Boolean engraving. Local bounded 20x20 threshold/grayscale recessed height field, not full 3D reconstruction; bright holes keep backing. .4–.6 mm depth, >=1 mm backing. STL has no units: confirm mm; orient, scale XY, set Z depth, rotate/place in safe radius6.5mm. Only one convex closed outward-wound broad flat-bottom solid <=1,500 triangles, <=2MB, .2–250mm per axis supported. Concavity, cavities, open/nonmanifold or intersecting meshes rejected, never convex-hulled or silently repaired. All files local. JSON saves bounded portable payloads.
7. Reference: White Cat Kit Card by Mike Kasberg, CC BY4.0, https://github.com/mkasberg/white-cat-kit-card / Printables865769. Measured1.60mm sheet/carrier and .21–.22mm runner architecture adapted to new beam/shuttle bays with strengthened runners. Source4.50x1.700mm sockets not used: cord eyes replace rigid joints. No raw reference mesh redistributed. Connected card, four clean-part STLs, JSON and this guide in ZIP. STL stores no colours. Computational manifold, assembly/collision, eye-wall, self-stress and tab-release checks are distinct from unperformed physical testing.
