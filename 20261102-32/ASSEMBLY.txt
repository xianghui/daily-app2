Stave & Halo — eight-part dry-vessel kit card.
1. Print flat at 100% millimeters, PLA/PETG, .4 mm nozzle, .16–.20 mm layers, three walls. All eight parts and the connected card are bed-flat 1.6–2.4 mm sheets. No supports or hardware intended. Physical printing, material strength and assembly are NOT tested. Small parts and snips require care.
2. Cut THIRTY-TWO low runners 1.3 mm outside each part bounding edge. File remaining stubs flush without damaging tongues, windows or collar sockets. Clean part exports omit runners. A 2.4 mm carrier grid joins all parts by true Boolean unions; this is not loose parts next to a frame.
3. Identify the solid FLOOR, open TOP HALO and six identical windowed STAVES. Dry-fit one stave tongue in one floor socket first. Source sheet 1.60 mm and source socket 4.50 x 1.70 mm informed this reconstruction. Proposed slots are 4.50 x (sheet + selected .30–.60) mm. Tongue width is 4.50 minus clearance; tongue length and thickness equal sheet. These are loose adhesive seats, NOT guaranteed press fits. Tune for elephant foot and material using one clean floor and stave before printing a full card.
4. Floor on the table: insert six lower tongues vertically until the broad shoulders rest on its top. Their ends are flush with the underside. Walls are tangent to a circle of radius outside diameter/2 minus 4.5 mm; broad windows and relieved corners remain open, not watertight. Use a little compatible adhesive in bottom sockets and a paper alignment jig or temporary tape while curing. Do not bend staves inward to fit.
5. Align all six top tongues with the TOP HALO sockets. Lower the halo straight down until its underside rests on all six upper shoulders; apply adhesive in these six sockets and let cure. Do not slide a halo over full-width stave shoulders. Top tongues end flush with halo top. Analytic assembly slider raises the halo along this real assembly path; exploded radial offsets are illustrative only. Exports always use the exact same geometry bed-flat.
6. This is a miniature dry decorative cup / short-pencils or dried-stems holder. Not food-safe, liquid-tight, a candle holder, load-rated or a structural container. No flame or hot object. Windows and open corner gaps are intentional. No motion or elastic snap function claimed. Geometry checks do not establish adhesive strength or stability when loaded. Start with lightweight contents; calibrate and physically test before use.
7. Personalization engraves the protected central floor, radius 6.5 mm, away from all twelve collar joints. PNG/JPEG/simple SVG become 20x20 threshold/grayscale relief, .4–.6 mm deep, leaving >=1 mm backing. Bright image holes retain backing. This is 2D-to-relief, not automatic full 3D photo reconstruction. Text and bundled icons use this same path. ASCII/binary STL input supports one closed outward-wound convex broad-flat-bottom solid <=1500 triangles, <=2 MB, .2–250 mm per axis. Unsupported concave/open/overhanging inputs are rejected, never convex-hulled or silently repaired. Confirm mm, set up-axis, footprint scale, Z depth, rotation and XY placement. The actual mesh is Boolean-subtracted, not merely previewed.
8. Files stay local. JSON and named designs contain bounded portable image/STL payloads. ZIP contains connected card, eight clean STLs, design JSON and these instructions with current fit dimensions. STL does not store preview colours. Reference: White Cat Kit Card by Mike Kasberg, CC BY4.0, https://github.com/mkasberg/white-cat-kit-card / Printables865769. Source sheet1.60 mm, socket4.50x1.700 mm, low runners.21–.22 mm were measured. New original six broad windowed staves, floor and halo reconstruct the reference's sheet-in-radial-socket principle; stronger .4–.6 mm runners, revised radial pitch and loose adhesive seats are design proposals. No source mesh redistributed. Computational manifold/clearance/assembly checks are not physical print tests.
