Savart & Sing — five-part sound-wheel kit card.
1. Print the connected card flat at 100% millimeters, PLA/PETG, .4 mm nozzle, .16–.20 mm layers and three walls. All parts are 1.6–2.4 mm bed-flat sheets. No supports intended. Print one clean upright and deck first to calibrate sockets and one wheel to check the M3 bore. Physical printing, rotation, tooth durability and sound have NOT been tested.
2. Snip TWENTY low runners 1.3 mm outside the part bounding edges. Trim/ file stubs flush without damaging wheel teeth or tongues. Wheel runners attach to tooth regions; clean part exports are recommended if perfectly even teeth matter. Frame and parts are genuinely Boolean joined, not a loose arrangement.
3. Seat the two double-tongued uprights in the four deck sockets, shoulders resting on the top. Tongues finish flush beneath. The measured reference used 1.60 mm sheet and 4.50 ×1.70 mm sockets. These proposed loose seats use socket4.50 ×(sheet+clearance), tongue4.50-clearance by sheet. Selected total clearance is .30–.60 mm; glue the four seats after a successful dry fit. Adhesive is required, not optional snap retention. Let cure fully.
4. Hardware: two M3×16 mm bolts, two M3 nyloc nuts, enough M3 washers to obtain the selected 1–2 mm axial clearance, two paper/card strips about 6×20 mm (0.15–0.3 mm thick), low-tack tape and plastic-compatible adhesive. Place a bolt through each upright from behind, add spacing washers, then a wheel, outer washer and nut. Adjust nut to retain the wheel without clamping it: the wheel turns independently around the bolt. Do not use a loose ordinary nut that can unwind. Bore is3.3–3.8 mm; gently deburr, never force. No hardware-free mode is claimed.
5. Tape one replaceable paper striker to each upright upper edge, extending forward to barely touch the rotating tooth tips. Bend/trim the paper to reach the tip circle, starting with only about0.2 mm radial overlap; the soft paper bends, NOT the printed teeth. Remove paper before checking free rotation. No predicted flex of printed PLA. If teeth catch, stop and reduce contact; do not motorize. Hold the deck while gently turning a wheel by its three finger holes; keep fingers away from striker contact. Small sharp teeth/fasteners require adult care.
6. Low wheel has N teeth, high wheel2N; equal angular speed gives a2:1 impact-rate ratio, f=N×rpm/60. Hand speeds are not equal or calibrated, sound can be a rough buzz rather than a musical note. This is a physical pulse-rate experiment, not an audio synthesizer or instrument accuracy claim. Assembled preview turns both wheels at equal speed only to explain geometry; bolt/washer/paper/tape are external and omitted. Exploded position is illustrative. Exports always remain bed-flat.
7. Local PNG/JPEG/simple SVG, text and icons become20×20 threshold/grayscale engraved height-field on the isolated deck apron, centered10 mm forward of the sockets, .4–.6 mm deep with at least1 mm backing. Bright holes remain uncut. This is2D-to-relief, not full3D photo reconstruction. Convex, broad flat-bottom, closed ASCII/binary STL≤1500 triangles,≤2 MB, .2–250 mm per axis is scaled into the same protected engraving zone and actually Boolean-subtracted. Confirm mm, choose up-axis, footprint size, depth, rotation and placement. Unsupported concave/open/overhang/self-intersecting input is rejected without hull replacement or claimed repair.
8. Files stay local. JSON and named designs include bounded portable payloads. Everything ZIP contains connected card, five clean STLs, current JSON and instructions. STL stores geometry, not colour. Reference: White Cat Kit Card, Mike Kasberg, CC BY4.0, https://github.com/mkasberg/white-cat-kit-card / Printables865769. Source sheet1.60 mm, socket4.50×1.700 mm and .21–.22 mm runners were inspected; original reconstruction uses4 matching sockets,2 tongues per upright and stronger.4–.6 mm runners. New toothed wheels and external running hardware are independent redesigns. No raw source mesh is redistributed. Computational checks are not physical tests.
