Polar & Project — two-part stereographic-projection kit card.
1. Print flat at 100% millimeter scale, PLA/PETG, .16–.20 mm layers, .4 mm nozzle, three perimeters. No supports intended. Inspect cord eyes and sockets in slicer. Computational checks only; no physical print or assembly testing.
2. Snip eight runners, four per part, 1.6 mm outside contours. Clean nubs without damaging tongues, eyes or plane. De-tabbed exports omit runners. The card is a real unioned connected solid.
3. Calibrate a tongue/socket first. Measured reference defaults: 1.60 mm sheet, 4.50 mm socket length. Socket 4.50 × (sheet+allowance); tongue (4.50−allowance) × sheet. Allowance 0.10–0.30 mm TOTAL, printer/material dependent, not universal press-fit. Lower ring upright through two base slots; shoulders stop at deck top. Bond two seats with compatible adhesive. Tongue depth equals the base thickness: tips finish flush at the underside. Shoulders stop on the top face; clean elephant foot before seating.
4. Supply three light cords/threads, each about 250 mm, low-tack tape, and compatible adhesive. Lay rays in front of the ring, not inside its solid plane. Secure with low-tack tape at the north and each corresponding meridian mark, then pass through matching base eyes and tie underneath. Base holes sit at y=−sheet/2−0.35mm for the front cord plane. Match increasing angles from south to increasingly distant targets. The preview shows ideal thin front-face ray centerlines only, not exported plastic. Tape/rim contact and cord thickness cause approximate alignment. Tie gently: ring is not a loaded frame. For zero-hardware geometric inspection leave cords out; aligned pierced sightlines still show the construction, but no physical visible rays.
5. This is stereographic projection from north onto the plane tangent at south, not an optical projector. At angle phi from south, meridian point=(R sin phi, R(1−cos phi)); base landing x=2R tan(phi/2). Three angles are step, 2×step, 3×step (step25–30°). Ring marks and plane targets are XZ-collinear; real cords are held in front with tape, not threaded along the solid ring plane. Their offsets/contact make physical lines approximate. No sphere surface is supplied: ring is a meridian section, not a full sphere. The north pole itself has no finite plane image.

Concave/open/nonmanifold/self-intersecting/unsupported meshes rejected, not repaired. Confirm mm, select orientation before attaching, scale XY proportionally and Z depth independently, rotate/center/place. Source and final geometry shown; bounded assets survive JSON/local save.
9. Connected card, assembled/exploded and isolated views share exact final geometry. Exploded offsets are illustrative. STL is bed-flat, geometry only, no colours.
